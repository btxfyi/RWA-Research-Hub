# RWA Research Hub

A public learning and research hub covering the tokenization of real-world assets (RWAs), the infrastructure that supports them, and the risks that matter when bringing off-chain assets on-chain.

## Why this repository exists

Real-world asset tokenization is more than issuing a token that represents something physical or financial. It involves legal rights, asset ownership, custody, servicing, valuation, data verification, smart contracts, transfer rules, and redemption processes.

This repository aims to explain those moving parts in clear language, document useful research, and build a practical reference for people exploring the RWA ecosystem.

## What you'll find here

- **RWA fundamentals** — key concepts, asset categories, participants, and terminology.
- **Tokenization lifecycle** — how an asset moves from off-chain structuring to on-chain issuance and ongoing management.

## A simplified view of tokenization

1. **Identify the asset:** define what is being represented and who owns it.
2. **Establish the legal structure:** document the rights attached to the token and the obligations of each party.
3. **Arrange custody and servicing:** determine who holds, manages, or administers the underlying asset.
4. **Verify information:** establish how ownership, value, payments, and other relevant events are confirmed.
5. **Issue the token:** deploy and test the smart contracts and issuance process.
6. **Manage the asset:** monitor the underlying asset, maintain records, and handle transfers, distributions, or redemption.

The exact process varies by asset class and jurisdiction. A token does not automatically give its holder direct legal ownership of an underlying asset.

## Research principles

- Prefer primary sources: legal documents, issuer disclosures, audits, official documentation, and regulatory publications.
- Separate verified facts from assumptions, estimates, and opinions.
- Include dates and links so readers can check whether information is current.
- Explain limitations, dependencies, and risks—not only product features.
- Never treat token price, projected yield, or marketing language as proof of asset quality.

## Important risks to examine

- Legal enforceability and the rights of token holders
- Custody, asset segregation, and insolvency protections
- Valuation methodology and update frequency
- Redemption terms, liquidity, and transfer restrictions
- Smart-contract, bridge, oracle, and key-management risks
- Counterparty, servicing, and operational dependencies
- Regulatory and jurisdictional uncertainty
