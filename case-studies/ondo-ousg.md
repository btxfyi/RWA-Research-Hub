# Case study: Ondo OUSG
**Snapshot:** 20 November 2026 | **Category:** Tokenized fund | **Status:** Desk research, not legal or investment due diligence

## Product and legal wrapper
OUSG is designed to provide eligible investors exposure to short-term US government securities. Ondo's product page describes the token as representing limited partnership interests in a US-domiciled 3(c)(7) fund offered under Regulation D Rule 506(c). This distinction matters: token holders should not be casually described as owning Treasury bills directly. The governing fund documents define the actual rights.

Official page: https://ondo.finance/ousg

## How the model works
1. An eligible investor completes onboarding and eligibility checks.
2. The investor subscribes and receives the relevant fund interest represented by OUSG.
3. The fund holds exposure to high-quality assets. The issuer page currently lists exposure that includes BlackRock's BUIDL, USDC and bank deposits.
4. Product information and third-party-administrator financial reporting are published.
5. Eligible holders request redemption under the current product terms.

Portfolio allocation, service providers, fees and settlement mechanics can change. Re-check the live documents.

## Access and liquidity snapshot
The product page lists a $5,000 minimum mint and redemption, and advertises instant minting/redemption with a 0% transaction fee. It also displays a 0.15% management fee as 0% until 1 January 2027, with fund expenses up to 0.15%. These are time-sensitive issuer-published terms, not permanent guarantees.

An advertised instant primary-market process is not the same as guaranteed secondary-market liquidity at every size or price.

## Diligence questions
- What exact legal claim does a token holder own?
- Which offering memorandum and partnership agreement govern?
- Who are the administrator, custodian, auditor and transfer agent?
- How are holdings valued and how frequently is NAV published?
- What happens if a service provider fails?
- Which eligibility, sanctions and transfer restrictions apply?
- What are the redemption cut-offs, settlement assets, limits and suspension rights?
- How do fees change after promotional periods?

## Balanced assessment
**Potential strengths:** defined regulated-fund wrapper, exposure to short-term government-related instruments, public product information and tokenized distribution.

**Dependencies and risks:** legal structure, underlying fund exposure, custody and administrator dependencies, eligibility restrictions, upgrade/admin controls, valuation, redemption operations and jurisdictional limits.

Tokenization changes distribution and recordkeeping rails; it does not remove legal, credit, market, operational or liquidity risk.
