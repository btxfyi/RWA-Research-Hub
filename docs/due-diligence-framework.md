# RWA Due Diligence Framework

Use this framework to evaluate an RWA issuer, protocol, tokenized fund, or product consistently. It is a research checklist—not a rating, certification, or investment recommendation.

## 1. Asset and backing

- What exactly is the underlying asset or portfolio?
- Is it identified individually or described only as a broad category?
- Who owns it, and what documents support that claim?
- Are there liens, senior claims, or other encumbrances?
- Is the asset diversified or concentrated?
- What happens if an asset is impaired or lost?

**Evidence to seek:** asset schedules, title or ownership records, audited financial statements, independent attestations, insurance details, and issuer disclosures.

## 2. Legal rights and structure

- What does each token legally represent?
- Who is the issuer and who is the obligor?
- Is there a bankruptcy-remote vehicle? If claimed, what supports that claim?
- What law governs the token-holder agreement?
- How are disputes handled?
- Can holders enforce rights directly, or only through an intermediary?

**Red flag:** vague claims such as “backed by real assets” without a clear explanation of holder rights.

## 3. Custody and counterparties

- Who holds the asset or authoritative records?
- Are client assets segregated?
- Can assets be rehypothecated, pledged, or lent?
- Who are the servicer, administrator, valuation agent, and auditor?
- Are replacement arrangements described?
- What concentration exists across service providers?

## 4. Valuation and transparency

- Is valuation based on market prices, models, appraisals, or issuer estimates?
- How frequently is NAV updated?
- Who independently reviews the methodology?
- Are liabilities, accrued fees, and impairments included?
- Can public reports be reconciled with on-chain supply?

Remember: proof of reserves, an audit, an attestation, and a valuation report answer different questions. None should be treated as a substitute for all the others.

## 5. Smart contracts and blockchain

- Which chain and token standard are used?
- Are contracts verified and independently audited?
- Can administrators mint, burn, pause, freeze, or upgrade?
- Who controls privileged keys, and are controls multisignature or otherwise protected?
- Are bridges involved?
- What happens during chain congestion, a fork, or an exploit?

## 6. Liquidity and redemption

- Is there a contractual redemption right?
- Who must honor it?
- What are the notice periods, fees, gates, minimums, and settlement timelines?
- Can redemption be suspended?
- Is secondary-market liquidity merely expected, or supported by a committed mechanism?
- Does the product have maturity or lock-up constraints?

**Important:** secondary-market trading availability is not the same as guaranteed redemption by the issuer.

## 7. Economics and cash flows

- Where does yield or return originate?
- Is it generated by asset cash flows, lending, trading, incentives, or a combination?
- What costs are deducted?
- Who absorbs defaults, losses, and expenses?
- Is the advertised rate historical, target, variable, or contractual?
- Are token incentives being presented separately from underlying asset income?

## 8. Regulatory and investor eligibility

- Which entities and jurisdictions are involved?
- Are there investor qualification or geographic restrictions?
- What transfer restrictions apply?
- What disclosures are provided?
- Which regulatory registrations, exemptions, or licenses are claimed, and can they be independently checked?

Do not infer regulatory approval from a company registration or a service provider's license.

## 9. Operational resilience

- Is there a business-continuity plan?
- How are incidents disclosed?
- Are key-person and vendor dependencies clear?
- Is there a process for correcting inaccurate data?
- What happens if the issuer or servicer stops operating?

## Evidence grading

Use a consistent label for every material claim:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified primary source | Directly supported by an official filing, contract, audit, or on-chain record |
| Independent corroboration | Confirmed by a credible independent source |
| Issuer-reported | Stated by the issuer but not independently verified |
| Analyst interpretation | Reasoned conclusion based on available evidence |
| Unverified | Evidence is missing, inaccessible, or inconclusive |
