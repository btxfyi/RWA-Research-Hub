# RWA Asset-Class Map

Different asset classes have different legal, operational, valuation, and liquidity requirements. A tokenization model that works for a short-term government security may not work for a building, a private loan, or a commodity.

| Asset class | Typical underlying cash flow or value driver | Important diligence areas |
|---|---|---|
| Government securities | Coupon, discount accretion, maturity repayment | Issuer risk, custody, duration, fund structure, settlement |
| Money-market funds | Income from short-term debt instruments | Fund documents, NAV, liquidity, gates, eligible holdings |
| Private credit | Borrower interest and principal repayments | Underwriting, defaults, collateral, servicing, recovery |
| Trade finance / receivables | Invoice or trade obligation repayment | Invoice validity, debtor concentration, fraud, payment collection |
| Real estate | Rent, property sale proceeds, or fund distributions | Title, SPV structure, occupancy, expenses, valuation, local law |
| Commodities | Commodity price and storage or delivery rights | Allocation, title, vaulting, insurance, audits, redemption |
| Corporate bonds | Coupon and principal repayment | Credit risk, seniority, covenants, default process |
| Carbon credits | Delivery or retirement of eligible credits | Registry, methodology, additionality, permanence, double counting |
| Infrastructure | Project revenues or contractual payments | Concession rights, construction, regulation, counterparty exposure |
| Equities / funds | Dividends and economic exposure | Issuer rights, corporate actions, tracking, custody, securities rules |

## Three distinct tokenization models

### 1. Token represents the asset itself

The legal structure recognizes the token or its associated registry entry as the record of ownership. The exact effect depends on applicable law and documentation.

### 2. Token represents an interest in a legal vehicle

A fund, trust, SPV, or similar entity holds the assets. The token represents an interest in that vehicle, subject to its governing documents.

### 3. Token represents a contractual claim or exposure

The holder has a claim against an issuer or counterparty. The holder may not own the underlying asset directly.

These are simplified categories. Actual products can combine features of multiple models.

## Asset-specific questions

**Government securities**
- Is the token backed by direct holdings, fund shares, or a contractual claim?
- Who is the custodian?
- How are accrued interest and expenses reflected in NAV?
- What are settlement and redemption cut-off times?

**Private credit**
- Who originates and underwrites loans?
- What is the borrower concentration?
- Are loans secured, and how is collateral perfected?
- Who services collections and handles defaults?
- What is the recovery process?

**Real estate**
- Does the token represent property title, SPV shares, or a contractual interest?
- Who controls property management?
- How are rent, vacancies, maintenance, and taxes reported?
- How often are appraisals updated?

**Commodities**
- Is metal or another commodity allocated to specific holders or pooled?
- Are serial numbers or bar lists available where relevant?
- Who insures and audits storage?
- Can holders redeem physically, and under what conditions?

**Receivables**
- Is the invoice genuine and accepted by the debtor?
- Has it been assigned or financed elsewhere?
- Who verifies delivery and payment obligations?
- How are disputes and late payments handled?

## Research rule

Never evaluate an RWA product only by its asset category. Evaluate the specific legal claim, asset pool, counterparties, operational process, and redemption terms.
