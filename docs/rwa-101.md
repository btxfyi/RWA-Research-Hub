# RWA 101: A Practical Introduction

## What is a real-world asset?

A real-world asset (RWA) is an asset or financial claim that exists outside a blockchain but is represented, referenced, or managed through blockchain-based infrastructure.

Examples include:
- Government securities and money-market instruments
- Corporate debt and private credit
- Real estate interests
- Commodities such as gold
- Funds and other investment products
- Receivables, invoices, and trade-finance claims

The important question is not simply whether an asset has a token. It is what legal or economic rights that token gives its holder.

## What does tokenization mean?

Tokenization is the process of creating a digital token that represents an asset, a beneficial interest, a contractual claim, or another defined right.

A token may provide:
- Direct ownership, where legally supported
- A beneficial interest through a trust, fund, or special-purpose vehicle
- A contractual claim against an issuer
- Exposure to an asset's economic performance
- Access or usage rights

These structures are not interchangeable. Read the offering documents to understand which one applies.

## The main participants

| Participant | Typical responsibility |
|---|---|
| Asset originator | Sources or creates the underlying asset |
| Issuer | Creates and offers the token or investment product |
| Legal vehicle | Holds assets or defines investor rights |
| Custodian | Safeguards assets or key records |
| Servicer | Collects payments and administers the asset |
| Valuation provider | Estimates asset value using a stated methodology |
| Transfer agent / registry | Maintains ownership records where applicable |
| Technology provider | Operates smart contracts and supporting systems |
| Oracle / attestor | Supplies external data or attestations |
| Investor / holder | Acquires the rights described in the legal documents |

One company may perform several roles. That concentration can create dependencies worth examining.

## Why use blockchains?

Potential benefits include:
- Programmable issuance and transfer rules
- Faster reconciliation between systems
- Transparent transaction histories
- Composability with other on-chain applications, where permitted
- Fractional denominations
- Automated distribution logic

These benefits are not automatic. They depend on legal design, integrations, liquidity, data quality, and operational controls.

## What tokenization does not automatically solve

A blockchain record does not, by itself:
- Prove that the underlying asset exists
- Guarantee that the issuer owns the asset
- Give a holder enforceable ownership rights
- Guarantee redemption at par or at a stated value
- Create a liquid secondary market
- Remove counterparty, custody, legal, or servicing risk

## Questions to ask before evaluating an RWA product

1. What exact asset or claim backs the token?
2. Who legally owns the underlying asset?
3. What rights does a token holder have?
4. Who holds custody, and how is segregation handled?
5. How are valuation and asset data verified?
6. How are income, fees, and losses allocated?
7. What are the transfer and eligibility restrictions?
8. How does redemption work, and who is obligated to pay?
9. What happens if the issuer, custodian, or servicer fails?
10. Which jurisdictions and regulations apply?

## Key distinction

**Token representation is not the same as legal ownership.** Always identify the legal claim first, then evaluate the blockchain implementation.
